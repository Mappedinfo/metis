# research-question

[中文主稿](SKILL.zh.md). The Chinese guide is the authored source and this
entry is its synchronized English translation. Both carry the same workflow,
boundaries, requirements and budgets; the discovery filename grants no language
semantic priority. Sources and changes: [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md).

## When to use

Use when the question, significance, testability or feasibility remains
unresolved, or when comparing research candidates. Use `research-taskbook` to
execute the planning decomposition after the question is accepted. Use
`research-story` when supplied results already support a settled question and
the work is narrative/evidence arrangement. Do not make attractive prose stand
in for a weak research question, or reject a question merely because its current
wording is weak.

## Method

1. Diagnose the starting object: a topic, claimed gap, hypothesis, method idea
   or decision problem. Write the current question in terms of an unknown and
   the conditions under which an answer matters. From the request resolve
   intended audience, available evidence, time and resources; ask only for a
   missing constraint that changes the candidate set. A domain term is not a
   question and a new method name is not a contribution.
2. Establish the minimal evidence base. Inspect supplied authoritative sources
   and identify the closest existing answer or realistic alternative. If a
   current field claim is decisive, use authorized scholarly retrieval to check
   it. Separate source-supported facts, inferences and assumptions; no result in
   a limited search is not proof nobody has studied the problem. Missing access
   keeps novelty or feasibility provisional, not automatically rejected.
3. Generate or repair candidate questions using the relevant tensions: a hidden
   assumption; rival explanations; a failure boundary; a simpler alternative;
   changed conditions that make an old limit testable; a decision harmed by
   systematic error. Use several lenses only if comparison is requested; a
   single question repair need not generate a long idea list.
4. For each serious candidate, test five things together: the stake (who or what
   gains knowledge/capability); the closest alternative (what is already known);
   the increment (what an answer could change); the falsifier (what observation,
   proof or case would weaken it); and the feasible check (what available
   materials could discriminate). A novel definition, isolated constructed
   example or gap claim is insufficient unless its consequence is explained.
   Select evidence standards by method: proof and counterexample for theory,
   fair protocol and uncertainty for experiments, identification assumptions
   for causal claims, materials and competing readings for interpretive work.
5. Stress-test the strongest candidate with the most consequential rival and
   rejection risk. Design the smallest informative pilot rather than a large
   implementation: name inputs, comparison/control, observable output, decision
   rule and the action each outcome would justify. Distinguish a planned check
   from completed evidence. Set thresholds from an accepted scale or variability
   estimate; otherwise keep the decision provisional. Do not demand experiments
   for a theoretical or source-analysis question.
6. Recommend pursue, repair, park or reject with reasons tied to evidence and
   constraints. Unresolved evidence is a named next check, not a made-up score
   or proof of failure. Preserve potentially useful negative outcomes and scope
   boundaries. A question card contains the question, stake, nearest alternative,
   proposed increment, assumptions/rivals, falsifier, feasible check, resources,
   rejection risk and next decision. Use a shorter answer for a narrow request;
   these are inspection fields, not compulsory reader-facing headings.

Maintain one requirements ledger for the accepted task and R1–R6 below. Record
`done`, `blocked` or `n/a` with an inspected artifact/location or a scope reason;
`n/a` cannot waive a requested outcome. A plan does not prove execution.

## Requirements

- [B] R1 A question is distinguished from topic/method/plan — inspect the
  unknown, conditions and expected answer type.
- [B] R2 Stakes and increment are defensible — compare with the closest known
  answer or alternative and label unresolved value/novelty claims.
- [B] R3 Evidence and assumptions stay separate — follow decisive source claims
  to checked locations and bound absence claims by actual retrieval.
- [B] R4 A meaningful falsifier and feasible check exist — test whether rival
  outcomes would change the recommendation without inventing evidence.
- [B] R5 The verdict matches resources and uncertainty — compare the decision,
  strongest risk and next action with supplied constraints.
- [A] R6 Alternatives or useful negative outcomes inform the choice — show the
  tradeoff when comparison is requested or can materially change the decision.

## When to stop

Use one candidate-generation/repair pass, one bounded evidence batch for
specified decisive gaps when authorized, and one stress-test/repair pass.
Deliver when the requested recommendation and applicable blocking checks pass.
When support remains absent at the cap, deliver provisional cards and identify
the smallest source, resource estimate or human decision needed. Keep required
novelty/value determinations blocked instead of presenting a mature verdict.

## Output contract

Deliver the requested question cards or focused assessment, the relevant
source/alternative comparison, a pursue/repair/park/reject recommendation with
its discriminating next check, and the requirements ledger. No state update,
experiment launch or downstream implementation is implied by recommending a
question. Attribution and adaptation are recorded in [PROVENANCE.md](PROVENANCE.md).
