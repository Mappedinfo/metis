# Local-reading and full-text boundary exercise

All material below is fictional. A six-page paper, *Predicting Small-Sample
Route Waiting Times*, is supplied only as pages 2–3. Page 2 Figure 2 reports
mean errors of 4.2 minutes for model A and 4.0 for B on twelve stops. Its caption
calls these descriptive means for the same stops and supplies no error bars or
test. Page 3 says: “Errors were evaluated on these twelve stops; observation
counts differed between stops.” Fitting and stop-weighting details are unknown.

1. “Does Figure 2 prove B is significantly better?” Use `question`: B has a
   lower displayed mean, but significance is unsupported. Cite Figure 2 and
   page 3, identifying weighting/uncertainty limits. Do not impose eight full
   sections or invent a test.
2. Neighboring request: “Closely read the entire paper and organize all its
   references.” Use `full`. Useful partial notes are possible, but missing
   pages 1 and 4–6 and the reference list keep full R1 and complete-report/
   reference outcomes blocked. A completed local answer does not cover them.
3. “Annotate the method passage with conditions that could affect the mean.”
   Without an authorized PDF-comment tool, return page, exact quote and the
   weighting question as a location-indexed note. State that the PDF was not
   edited. Unknown weighting is a question, not a confirmed methodological flaw.

A second theoretical abstract says a coloring exists for finite undirected
graphs under stated degree constraints, with proof/counterexamples in unavailable
full text. Asked for contribution type, tentatively identify theory from the
abstract and disclose unverified proof; do not require ML baselines or certify it.

These exercises cover mode, coverage and claim boundaries, not PDF/OCR tool
availability or fresh-host skill loading.
